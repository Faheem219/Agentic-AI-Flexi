# How does spaced repetition affect long-term retention in learning? (baseline)

Spaced repetition is a learning technique that involves reviewing information at increasing intervals, a method robustly supported by cognitive psychology to enhance long-term retention. The foundational principle, often traced back to Ebbinghaus's forgetting curve, posits that memory decay is non-linear and can be counteracted by strategically timed retrieval practice [1]. Research consistently demonstrates that spacing out study sessions leads to superior retention compared to massed practice, a phenomenon known as the spacing effect [2]. This effect is not merely about repetition but about the cognitive effort required to retrieve information after a delay, which strengthens memory traces and facilitates deeper encoding [3]. Modern applications of spaced repetition, often implemented via digital flashcard systems, utilize algorithms to optimize these intervals based on individual performance, ensuring that items are reviewed just before they are likely to be forgotten [4]. Empirical studies in various educational contexts, including medical education and language learning, confirm that spaced repetition significantly outperforms traditional study methods for both short-term testing and long-term knowledge maintenance [5]. Furthermore, the benefits of spaced repetition are amplified when combined with active recall, where learners are forced to generate the answer rather than passively rereading material [6]. Recent neuroscientific evidence suggests that spaced repetition modulates neural activity in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex, promoting the consolidation of information into long-term memory stores [7]. Despite its efficacy, the technique requires sustained learner engagement and meta-cognitive awareness to be effective, as learners often underestimate the value of spacing and prefer the illusion of competence provided by massed study [8]. Ultimately, spaced repetition remains one of the most reliable pedagogical tools for maximizing durable learning, transforming transient exposure into stable, retrievable knowledge.
