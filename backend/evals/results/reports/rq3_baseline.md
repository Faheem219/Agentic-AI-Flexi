# What are the known health effects of microplastic exposure in humans? (baseline)

The ubiquity of microplastics (MPs) in the environment has led to inevitable human exposure via ingestion, inhalation, and dermal contact. While research is still evolving, current evidence suggests that MPs pose significant risks to human health, primarily through physical irritation, chemical toxicity, and the potential for inflammatory responses. Studies have confirmed the presence of microplastics in human blood, lung tissue, and the placenta, indicating systemic distribution [1, 2]. One of the primary concerns is the inflammatory response triggered by the physical presence of these particles. In vitro and animal models demonstrate that MPs can induce oxidative stress and cytotoxicity, potentially leading to chronic inflammation, which is a precursor to various metabolic and respiratory diseases [3, 4]. Furthermore, microplastics act as vectors for hazardous chemicals. They can adsorb persistent organic pollutants (POPs), heavy metals, and endocrine-disrupting chemicals from the surrounding environment, which are then released into the human body upon ingestion [5]. The potential for these particles to cross biological barriers, such as the blood-brain barrier or the placental barrier, raises concerns regarding neurotoxicity and developmental impacts [6]. Epidemiological data remains limited, but emerging research suggests a correlation between high microplastic exposure and increased risks of inflammatory bowel disease and other gastrointestinal disorders [7]. Additionally, the inhalation of airborne microplastics has been linked to respiratory issues, including airway inflammation and potential lung tissue damage [8]. Despite these findings, establishing definitive causal links in humans remains challenging due to the heterogeneity of microplastic types, sizes, and the complexity of exposure pathways. Future research must focus on standardized quantification methods and longitudinal studies to better elucidate the long-term health consequences of chronic microplastic exposure.
