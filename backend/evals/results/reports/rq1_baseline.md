# What techniques are most effective at reducing hallucinations in retrieval-augmented generation systems? (baseline)

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged as a primary solution to mitigate hallucinations in Large Language Models (LLMs) by grounding responses in external knowledge [4]. However, RAG systems themselves are susceptible to hallucinations if the retrieved information is irrelevant, contradictory, or if the model fails to adhere to the provided context. Recent research has identified several highly effective techniques to address these challenges. One prominent approach is the implementation of self-correction mechanisms. Asai et al. introduced Self-RAG, a framework where the model learns to output reflection tokens that evaluate the relevance of retrieved documents and the factual support of its own generated segments [1]. This allows the system to critique its output dynamically. Similarly, Yan et al. proposed Corrective Retrieval-Augmented Generation (CRAG), which utilizes a lightweight retrieval evaluator to assess the quality of retrieved documents, triggering a web search if the internal knowledge base is insufficient [2]. Another effective strategy involves iterative and multi-stage retrieval. Instead of a single-step retrieval process, techniques like ITER-RETGEN leverage the synergy between generation and retrieval, using the model's initial outputs to refine subsequent queries, thereby narrowing the gap between the information needed and the information retrieved [5]. This iterative process ensures that the context remains highly relevant to the specific nuances of the user's query. Furthermore, the integration of structured knowledge, such as Knowledge Graphs (KGs), helps reduce hallucinations by providing explicit relational data that unstructured text might lack. Pan et al. highlight that combining LLMs with KGs can provide a ground truth anchor that prevents the model from making false associative leaps [7]. Additionally, improving the reranking stage of the RAG pipeline is crucial. Gao et al. emphasize that advanced rerankers ensure the most pertinent information is placed within the LLM's context window, reducing the likelihood of the model hallucinating based on noise in the top-k results [3]. Finally, enforcing strict attribution and citation has been shown to improve faithfulness. The ALCE benchmark demonstrates that requiring models to provide citations for their claims forces a higher degree of adherence to the source material [6]. Collectively, these techniques transition RAG from a static retrieval process to a dynamic, self-aware framework.
